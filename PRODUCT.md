# First Principles — Product Specification

## Product thesis

Do not simply teach children advanced mathematics earlier. Give them the **intuition earlier**.

First Principles is an interactive learning environment for elementary-age children that makes advanced ideas familiar through visual, tactile, game-like experiences before school introduces the formal notation. The experience should feel closer to a great puzzle game than a digital textbook.

## Core learning loop

Each required lesson should usually take 2–4 minutes:

1. Hook / wonder
2. Manipulate / play
3. Predict
4. Discover
5. Brief explanation, name, or notation
6. 2–4 tactile challenges
7. Transfer the idea to a different representation or context
8. Reward / unlock

Before creating a multiple-choice question, ask whether the learner can demonstrate the answer by moving, building, launching, matching, sorting, drawing, rotating, folding, measuring, pouring, or navigating. If yes, use the action.

## Ten product principles

1. Expose before explaining.
2. Interact before calculating.
3. Demonstrate instead of selecting A/B/C/D whenever possible.
4. Show mistakes visually and immediately, then allow another attempt.
5. Teach the same idea in many contexts and representations.
6. Exercise foundational CogAT-style reasoning continuously.
7. Keep required lessons approximately 2–4 minutes.
8. Reward mastery, transfer, persistence, and connections more than repetition.
9. Make accumulated knowledge visible through the learner's world.
10. As learners mature, make access to more interesting ideas increasingly become the reward itself.

## Curriculum architecture

Use a knowledge graph rather than grade-level textbook units. Advancement is based primarily on demonstrated readiness and mastery.

Example concept progression:

number line → negative numbers → multiplication as scaling → fractions → ratios → proportions → unknown quantities → variables → coordinates → patterns → functions → angles → distance → speed → vectors

Visible learning worlds may include Numbers, Relationships, Shapes, Space, Patterns, Chance, Motion, Forces, Change, and Infinity.

Advanced ideas should appear early as intuitive puzzles without unnecessary formalism: gravity and black holes, vectors through spacecraft movement, energy through roller coasters, torque through bridges, functions through input/output machines, area/integration through accumulation, probability, combinatorics, and more.

## Foundational reasoning / CogAT framework

CogAT preparation is an important outcome but not the entire curriculum. Treat CogAT-style reasoning as foundational cognitive infrastructure that feeds mathematics and science.

Track at least these reasoning dimensions:

1. Pattern recognition
2. Analogical reasoning
3. Classification
4. Part-to-whole reasoning
5. Quantity relationships
6. Sequencing
7. Spatial transformation
8. Composition and decomposition
9. Cause and effect
10. Constraint solving

Support CogAT-style activity families including analogies, sentence completion where age appropriate, classification, number analogies, number puzzles, number series, figure matrices, paper folding, and figure classification.

The child experience should be games rather than test-prep screens. Parent-facing surfaces may explicitly show CogAT-relevant skill coverage.

## Dual mastery model

Track both:

- Knowledge mastery: fractions, ratios, geometry, coordinates, algebra, physics, etc.
- Reasoning mastery: analogies, pattern recognition, spatial reasoning, classification, quantitative reasoning, etc.

Diagnose the source of difficulty. For example, if a learner understands fractions in direct tasks but struggles when fractions appear inside analogies, train analogical reasoning using already-known content rather than merely drilling more fractions.

## Concept transfer

Repeated exposure should deliberately present the same underlying structure in different contexts.

Example equivalence chain:

1/4 pie → 1 out of 4 → 25/100 → 0.25 → 25% → 1:4

Then transfer it into cooking, measurement, money, time, angles, maps, construction, games, and science.

Use connection challenges such as asking what 1/4 of a pie, $0.25 of $1, 15 minutes of an hour, and 90 degrees of a circle have in common.

## Reusable tactile game engines

Initial reusable mechanics:

- Build / Stack — fractions, area, ratios, equations, spatial reasoning
- Match — equivalent forms, classifications, analogies, patterns
- Launch — number lines, coordinates, angles, trajectories
- Pour — fractions, decimals, volume, proportions
- Fold / Cut — symmetry, geometry, paper-folding reasoning

Later engines:

- Balance
- Fit / Rotate
- Sequence
- Navigate
- Sandbox / experiment

Game mechanics should be familiar and satisfying, drawing inspiration from puzzle, construction, physics, and matching games without copying protected branding or assets.

## Adaptive tutor

Children help train the tutor. Provide fast controls such as:

- Too easy
- Make it harder
- Too challenging
- Make it more fun
- Show me another way
- Surprise me

Difficulty and engagement are separate dimensions. “Make it more fun” should preserve cognitive difficulty while changing mechanic, context, theme, pacing, or presentation. “Too hard” may mean increasing visual scaffolding rather than abandoning the concept.

Do not increase difficulty merely by using larger numbers. Increase abstraction, number of constraints, transfer distance, or number of interacting rules.

The adaptive model should consider mastery, challenge, engagement, recent attempts, transfer performance, and explicit child feedback.

## Rewards and confidence

Desired loop:

Curiosity → discovery → “I get it” → successful application → mastery → meaningful reward → visible progress → confidence → curiosity

Reward learning rather than grinding. Replaying already-mastered easy content should produce little or no progression currency.

Reward events may include:

- First Try
- Aha!
- Explorer
- Breakthrough
- Second Try
- Comeback
- Different Way
- Connection
- No Hint
- Mastery Bonus
- Transfer Bonus

Trying a difficult concept again should be positively reinforced. Success after struggle can earn more meaningful recognition than immediate success. Consider special Breakthrough Buildings that are unlocked only by mastering concepts that initially caused difficulty.

## Persistent world

The long-term reward layer is a buildable city/world inspired by construction and simulation games.

Possible progression:

Village → Town → City → Metropolis → Planet → Solar System

The world should increasingly use the mathematics the learner has mastered. Example unlocks:

- Bridge: angles + measurement + forces
- Space Center: coordinates + ratios + velocity + gravity
- Skyscraper: area + perimeter + multiplication + proportions
- Restaurant: fractions + ratios + measurement
- Factory: variables + input/output + functions
- Carnival: probability

The city becomes a visual autobiography of learning. Tapping a landmark should eventually show the ideas that helped unlock it.

Primary progression currency must come from learning; parents should not be able to purchase educational mastery.

## Themes

Separate curriculum from presentation. A lesson should have internal metadata such as concept, reasoning skill, CogAT skill, difficulty, interaction type, scaffolding level, and transfer contexts. The theme engine changes story, objects, art, sound, and game framing without changing the essential learning objective.

Initial themes:

- Block building
- Animal care
- Fantasy / princess
- Cars / racing
- Space

Future themes may include dinosaurs, sports, cooking, art, ocean, construction, trains, nature, robots, medicine/health, and game creation.

Allow learners eventually to mix themes, such as animals + space or cars + building.

When using references to commercial games such as Minecraft or Roblox, do not use protected branding/assets without appropriate rights. Generic block-building and game-creator themes are acceptable.

## Accessibility and read-aloud

Read-aloud is a first-class app-wide capability, not a buried accessibility mode.

Requirements:

- speaker control for every instructional text
- optional Auto-Read
- short conversational spoken prompts
- replay without penalty
- slow and normal reading speeds
- never require reading to understand a game when visual demonstration can carry the instruction
- speak mathematical notation naturally
- narrate spatial actions while visually demonstrating them

Future controls can include Read More to Me, I Want to Read It Myself, Read Slower, and Say That Again.

The app should measure reasoning and mathematics rather than accidentally measuring reading ability.

## Safety / child design

Keep experiences age-appropriate. Health or animal-care themes should use fictional, educational scenarios rather than real clinical instructions. Avoid dark patterns, punitive streak mechanics, manipulative monetization, public social pressure, and systems that make children anxious about losing progress.

## MVP definition

The first meaningful MVP should target:

- 1 local child profile
- 5 themes
- 10–15 polished lessons initially, growing toward ~50
- 5 reusable tactile game engines initially
- read-aloud across all learner-facing prompts
- adaptive challenge/fun controls
- dual mastery tracking
- coins/rewards
- a small buildable city
- CogAT/reasoning tags behind lessons
- persistent local progress
- installable PWA
- mobile packaging path through Capacitor

## Success metric

The most important early qualitative question: when a 2–4 minute lesson ends, does the child voluntarily choose **one more**?
